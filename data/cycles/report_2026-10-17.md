# Weekly Performance & Science Report

### 1. What We Now Believe
* **H1 (Cardiac drift):** Heart rate drifts upward within rides at matched power (+4.3 bpm/h) — **94.6% belief**.
* **H2 (Heat-drift interaction):** Cardiac drift is exacerbated by heat — **20.6% belief**.
* **H3 (Sleep & descending):** Sleep restriction impairs technical descent speeds — **94.5% belief**.
* **H4 (Fitness time constant):** Aerobic fitness adapts faster than 42 days ($\tau_1 < 42\text{ d}$) — **50.0% belief**.
* **H5 (Long-term aerobic efficiency):** Heart rate at climbing power decreased over 18 months — **13.8% belief**.
* **H6 (Temperature & HR):** Submaximal climbing HR rises directly with ambient temperature — **43.3% belief**.
* **H7 (Wet trail friction):** Wet trail conditions slow descent times — **65.0% belief**.
* **H8 (Circadian HR):** Climbing HR is systematically lower in afternoon rides — **61.6% belief**.
* **H9 (Intra-ride descent fatigue):** Descents slow down later in rides — **48.6% belief**.
* **H10 (Rapid fatigue clearance):** Acute training fatigue clears in under 7 days ($\tau_2 < 7\text{ d}$) — **55.0% belief**.
* **H11 (Sleep & climbing HR):** Short sleep depresses submaximal climbing heart rate — **35.5% belief**.

### 2. What the Data Showed This Week
* **Cardiac drift:** Strong intra-ride drift persists at mean $\gamma_d = 4.30\text{ bpm/h}$ (sd $2.10$).
* **Sleep & descent speed:** Each lost hour of sleep slows technical descents by $2.06\%$ ($\delta_{\text{sleep}} = -0.020$, sd $0.014$).
* **Sleep & climbing HR:** Rides following $\le 6.5\text{ h}$ sleep exhibited a $-2.95\text{ bpm}$ mean HR residual versus $-0.62\text{ bpm}$ when sleep was $> 6.5\text{ h}$ ($n=36$ vs $184$).
* **Long-term drift:** Baseline HR intercept drifted up $+10.93\text{ bpm}$ (sd $3.59$) across the historical record ($P(\text{decrease}) = 0.002$).

### 3. What We Could Not Determine & Why
* **Fitness vs. fatigue time constants ($\tau_1, \tau_2$):** Posterior overlap with prior is high ($\text{BC} = 0.836$ for $\tau_1$, $0.911$ for $\tau_2$). Normal training variation provides insufficient contrast to uncouple fatigue decay from chronic fitness gain.
* **Surface wetness ($\delta_{\text{wet}}$):** Estimate sits near zero (mean $-0.001$, sd $0.048$) due to limited rain trials under standard descent protocols.
